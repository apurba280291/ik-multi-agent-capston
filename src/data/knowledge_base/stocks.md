## What Is a Stock
A stock is a share of ownership in a company. If the company grows and becomes more profitable, the share price may rise, and some companies also pay dividends. If the company struggles, the price can fall and you can lose part or all of your investment. Stocks are traded on exchanges such as the NYSE and Nasdaq, and each company has a ticker symbol, for example AAPL for Apple.

## How Stock Prices Move
Prices change as buyers and sellers agree on new prices. Over the short term, news, earnings reports, interest-rate expectations and investor mood drive prices, which makes short-term moves hard to predict. Over the long term, prices tend to follow company earnings growth. This is why long-term investors focus on business quality and diversification rather than daily moves.

## Dividends and Dividend Yield
A dividend is a cash payment a company makes to shareholders, usually quarterly. The dividend yield is the annual dividend divided by the share price: a $2 annual dividend on a $50 stock is a 4% yield. A very high yield can signal that the market doubts the dividend will last. Dividends are typically taxable in a regular brokerage account, with qualified dividends usually taxed at lower rates.

## Market Capitalization: Large, Mid and Small Cap
Market capitalization is share price times shares outstanding. Large-cap companies (roughly above $10 billion) tend to be established and less volatile; mid-caps sit in between; small-caps (roughly under $2 billion) offer more growth potential and more risk. Many index funds are organised by market cap, which lets investors hold all sizes at once.

## Growth Stocks vs Value Stocks
Growth stocks are companies expected to grow earnings faster than average and often reinvest profits instead of paying dividends; their prices are usually high relative to earnings. Value stocks trade at lower prices relative to earnings or assets and often pay dividends. Neither style wins in every period, so many investors hold both through a broad index fund.

## Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
The P/E ratio is the share price divided by earnings per share. A P/E of 20 means investors pay $20 for each $1 of annual earnings. A high P/E can mean investors expect strong growth; a low P/E can mean a bargain or a troubled business. Compare P/E only with similar companies or the company's own history, not across very different industries.

## Stock Sectors and Industries
The market is divided into sectors such as technology, healthcare, financials, energy, consumer staples and utilities. Sectors behave differently across the economic cycle: defensive sectors like utilities and staples tend to hold up better in downturns, while cyclical sectors like technology and consumer discretionary tend to lead in expansions. Owning several sectors reduces the risk of one industry hurting your whole portfolio.

## Setting SMART Financial Goals
Good goals are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. "Save $30,000 for a house down payment in five years" is much more useful than "save for a house". A clear target and deadline let you compute the monthly saving required and choose a suitable investment mix.

## Matching Investments to Time Horizon
Money needed within about three years belongs in low-risk places such as savings accounts, CDs or short-term Treasuries, since a market drop could force you to sell at a loss. Goals five to ten years away may use a balanced mix, and goals more than ten years away can hold more stocks. As a goal approaches, gradually shift toward safer assets.

## The 50/30/20 Budget
The 50/30/20 rule splits after-tax income into 50% needs (housing, food, transport, minimum debt payments), 30% wants and 20% savings and extra debt payments. It is a starting point, not a strict rule: in high-cost cities needs may exceed 50%. Pay yourself first by automating transfers to savings and investments on payday.

## Paying Off Debt vs Investing
Compare the interest rate on the debt with the return you can reasonably expect from investing. Paying off a 22% credit card balance is a guaranteed 22% return and almost always comes first. For low-rate debt, such as a 3-4% mortgage, investing may make sense after capturing any employer match. Many people do some of both.

## Saving for a Down Payment
Down payment savings are a short-to-medium-term goal, so risk should be modest. A common benchmark is 20% down to avoid private mortgage insurance, though many loans accept less. Using a high-yield savings account, CDs or short-term bond funds protects against losing money right before you need it.

## Required Monthly Savings for a Goal
To estimate what to save monthly, work backward from the target amount, the number of years and an assumed annual return. At 6% annual growth, reaching $50,000 in 10 years with no starting balance takes roughly $300 a month. More conservative returns require more saving. Using a range of return assumptions shows how sensitive the result is.

## Building Net Worth
Net worth is assets minus liabilities. Tracking it quarterly shows progress better than looking at any single account. Increase it by raising income, lowering spending, paying down high-interest debt and consistently investing.

## Insurance as Part of a Plan
Insurance protects your plan from catastrophic events. Basic coverage includes health, disability, auto, renters or homeowners, and term life insurance if others depend on your income. Term life insurance is usually far cheaper than permanent policies for pure protection.
